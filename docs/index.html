Korea to Unveil 2040 Power Demand Forecast at Public Hearing Next Week # [SUBTITLE] National Assembly to host power-plan hearings on the 20th Power demand recalculated to reflect mega-projects Renewable energy rollout plan through 2040 also to be unveiled # [BYLINE] By Joo Jae-hyun # [ARTICLE] The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment will hold a public hearing next week on its 12th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand, which sets Korea's power supply blueprint through 2040. The session will unveil an upwardly revised power demand forecast — reflecting three major national projects and surging demand from the artificial intelligence boom — along with a plan for building renewable energy plants through 2040. The ministry said on the 15th that it will hold the fourth and fifth public policy debates on the 12th plan at Seminar Room 3 of the National Assembly Members' Office Building on the 20th. The electricity plan is the country's top-tier framework underpinning mid- to long-term power policy, covering power demand over the next 15 years, plans for the generation capacity needed to meet it, and plans for building transmission and substation networks. It has been drawn up every two years, but because the 11th plan was finalized last year — a year later than originally scheduled — the 12th plan is now being prepared in close succession. The fourth debate, beginning at 10 a.m. on the 20th, will discuss the direction of energy policy in response to the era of the "electricity state" ushered in by the AI revolution. The fifth debate, held the same afternoon, will revisit power demand projections through 2040 and review the renewable energy rollout plan. The ministry released its power demand forecast through 2040 — the basis for the 12th plan — in April, but it has since recalculated demand following the announcement of the three major national projects and a sharp rise in demand from AI data centers. The government's new demand forecast is expected to far exceed the earlier projection. In April, the ministry forecast that peak annual power demand in 2040, factoring in population change and economic growth, would reach 131.8 to 138.2 gigawatts (GW). Given that recent summer peak demand has run at around 95 to 97 GW, that projection implies power demand jumping more than 40% in about 15 years. The problem is that just two months after that forecast, the government announced its three major national projects — a plan to build four semiconductor fabrication plants in the southwestern region, the early construction of the Yongin semiconductor cluster, and the construction of large-scale AI data centers. The additional generation capacity needed for these alone comes to 24.7 GW, equivalent to 18 nuclear reactors of 1.4 GW each. With electric vehicles spreading faster than expected and the government pushing to expand heat pumps, power demand is bound to rise considerably. Power authorities will draw up their generation capacity plan based on the demand figures presented at the hearing. Among these, the renewable energy rollout plan will be unveiled at the hearing as well. How much of the non-renewable power demand will be met by new nuclear reactors will be discussed at a separate debate around September or October. The ministry also plans to hold hearings on a roadmap for phasing out coal-fired power and on the direction of electricity market reform. # [DISCLAIMER] This article was automatically translated from Korean using AI.
